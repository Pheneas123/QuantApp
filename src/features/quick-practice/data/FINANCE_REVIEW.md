## Accuracy and authoring decisions

- Each item was reviewed with its four alternatives, canonical answer and explanation. Prompts distinguish payoff from profit, physical from risk-neutral probabilities, present value from delivery price, and volatility from variance. Distractors target sign, frequency, missing-income, discounting or model-assumption errors.
- Numerical prompts specify compounding and timing. The accrued-interest question supplies the exact 90/360 accrual fraction. Other payment questions state accrual fractions directly; no unspecified day-count rule is inferred. FRA settlement explicitly discounts to the accrual period’s start.
- Recovery pricing fixes both state payments at maturity. CVA is a deliberately simplified unilateral one-date calculation. ECL uses fixed conditional loss assumptions; the scenario-dependent example demonstrates why averaging the factors separately is invalid. These are educational pricing/loss models rather than full accounting or capital implementations.
- Interest-rate swap pricing explicitly uses a single curve. Hull–White curve fit does not imply positivity or a perfect option fit. CIR positivity distinguishes nonnegative solutions from a sufficient boundary-inaccessibility condition. Numéraire valuation explicitly requires a true martingale relative-value process, avoiding an inference from local-martingale or integrability assumptions alone.
- Arbitrage examples assume executable trades and the required borrowing, fungibility or delivery conditions. The call butterfly tests nonnegative payoffs across all spot regions. Early-call exercise uses no dividends and positive interest rates; its result is not extended to dividend-paying assets or American puts.
- The discrete ES example specifies equally weighted tail scenarios, and the VaR counterexample explicitly defines its quantile. Gaussian square-root scaling states independence, identical distributions, zero mean and additive loss. FRTB tests the Basel model design rather than jurisdiction-specific legal implementation dates.
- Independent calculation checks caught and corrected the dealer’s conditional expected value: £100.40, plus £0.10 cost, means a £100.50 ask. No answer was accepted solely on the basis of the written explanation. The five answer-length flags from the first audit were reworded and the final audit has no such flags.
- Exact duplicate prompts/IDs are rejected by the combined runtime validator. An editorial comparison of related ideas distinguishes basic spread cost from multi-venue arbitrage, simple from compound interest, new-forward price from existing-forward value, and single-period loss from dependent-scenario loss. No cosmetic numerical variants were added. Token-similarity screening of Finance against the full bank flagged nine pairs above 0.32 token-set similarity (maximum 0.43). These were reviewed: zero pricing/spot pricing/bootstrap test valuation versus curve inference; risk-neutral credit pricing adds contingent recovery; carry versus cash-and-carry tests pricing versus exploiting a misquote; ECL versus CVA adds derivative exposure and discounting; dependent loss versus ES tests scenario expectation versus tail averaging. The two mathematics/OTC pairs shared generic words only. No duplicate objective was found. Token screening is an editorial aid, not proof that semantic duplicates cannot exist.
- All authored prose follows British English and uses the existing `String.raw`/MathText LaTeX conventions. Explanations refer to values or concepts, never shuffled answer letters. All 100 explanations are shown by the current component; no hidden `workedSolution` fields or new solution UI were introduced.
